The trained net in 3D. The long field is W₁ᵀ, {H} hidden units by {N} input slots; the {INPUT} lights a handful of its columns, and their sum flows through ReLU, W₂ and softmax to the answer. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, or press Glide.

How to read it

  1. Input x. The strip on the left has {N} slots. Each feature of the {INPUT} is hashed to one slot, which becomes 1; every other slot is 0.
  2. W₁ᵀ. The long field: one row of {N} weights for each of the {H} hidden units. Height and colour show each weight. Only the columns of the lit slots take part; the rest are dimmed. The weights themselves are fixed after training and are the same for every {INPUT}: a new {INPUT} lights different columns, it does not change the matrix.
  3. Σ = W₁ᵀx. The streams carry the lit columns forward, and they are added up: 32 numbers.
  4. + b₁ = z₁. The bias row is added. It is fixed, so it looks the same for every {INPUT}; every row is drawn to one scale for all of them.
  5. σ = ReLU. Negative entries become 0 (shown flat and grey). This bend is what makes a net more than one matrix.
  6. × W₂, then Σ + b₂ = z₂. Each survivor is multiplied by its two weights, for "{C0}" and "{C1}", and each column is added up.
  7. softmax → p. The two numbers become probabilities that add up to 1.
